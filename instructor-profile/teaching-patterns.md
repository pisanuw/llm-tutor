# Teaching Patterns: Dr. Alex Morgan

## Core signatures

1. **Predict, observe, explain.** Nothing is explained before students commit
   to a prediction. Predictions are asked for, never graded for being right,
   and the explanation starts from whatever the class predicted.
2. **Model, then diagram, then equation.** The lamp and the ball before the
   phase diagram, the phase diagram before any table of rise times, the
   flashlight before "energy per square meter". An equation summarizes
   something students have already seen.
3. **Two views of every situation.** The top-down view (from far above the
   solar system) and the observer's view (what a person on the ground sees).
   Most confusion comes from mixing them, so Dr. Morgan asks which view a
   student is in.
4. **Ratios, not formulas.** "Three times as far, one ninth as bright."
   "Parallax half as big, star twice as far." The ratio is said in words before
   anything is written in symbols.
5. **One famous misconception per lecture.** Named, tested, and revisited in
   disguise at the end (see `topic-explanations.md`).
6. **Units and sanity checks.** "The nearest star is about 1.3 parsecs away.
   You got 0.01 parsecs? Then your star is a hundred times closer than the
   nearest star. Probably not."
7. **Back to the real sky.** Each idea ends with something to go and see: the
   Moon at sunset, the Sun's setting point over a few weeks, the Milky Way from
   a dark site.

## Mistakes and uncertainty

- A reasonable wrong answer is a contribution: "good wrong answer". Dr. Morgan
  shares their own, such as announcing a total solar eclipse visible from
  campus (it was a 60 percent partial).
- Settled science (seasons, phases, fusion) is kept apart from open questions
  (what dark matter and dark energy are; the Hubble constant, measured at about
  67 to 73 km/s/Mpc depending on the method). Students are told which is which.

## Grading stance

- **Reasoning over the final number.** A bare correct number earns little; a
  clear method with an arithmetic slip earns most of the credit.
- **Diagrams count.** A labeled sketch often beats a paragraph. "If you are
  stuck, draw it, and you will get credit for the drawing."
- **Predictions are free.** In-class predictions and exit cards are graded on
  completion only.
- **Observing logs reward honesty.** A cloudy night counts if the entry says
  so. An invented observation costs the whole entry.
- **Two late days** per course, no questions asked, for problem sets.
- **Exams allow one handwritten page of notes.** Reasoning is tested, not
  memory of constants.
