# Lecture Flow: Dr. Alex Morgan

Every session is 75 minutes and follows the same arc, so students always know
where they are.

1. **Sky report (5 min).** What is up tonight: the Moon's phase and when it
   rises, a bright planet, one constellation to find. Ends with a small
   observing task ("Find the Moon tonight and note the time"). Those
   observations become next session's examples.
2. **Recap question (5 min).** One question on last session's big idea,
   answered on paper before anyone speaks ("Draw the Moon at first quarter").
   Two different answers go on the board and the class settles it.
3. **The prediction (5 min).** The day's central question, posed before any
   teaching: "Why is it hot in summer?" Students commit by show of hands. The
   tally stays on the board all session.
4. **Demonstration (10 to 15 min).** A physical model whenever possible: the
   lamp and the ball, the flashlight, thumbs for parallax. Students do it
   themselves where they can. It confirms or breaks the tally.
5. **Explanation (15 to 20 min).** Built from the demonstration. Top-down view
   first, then the observer's view, numbers last and as ratios. The slides
   carry the definitions; the talk carries the reasoning.
6. **Pair problem (10 min).** One explains, one checks. Round numbers, so
   attention goes to reasoning. Dr. Morgan collects one good wrong answer to
   share.
7. **Misconception check (5 min).** Back to the tally: "Who wants to change
   their vote?" Then one question that catches the old idea in disguise.
8. **Wrap-up (5 min).** Three-line summary, an exit question on a card (the
   same shape as next session's recap), and the week's observing task.

## Pacing rules

- Never more than 15 minutes of talk without a question to the room.
- Define a term in plain words before its name appears.
- Running behind: cut the second worked example, never the prediction or the
  misconception check.
