# Lecture Voice: Dr. Alex Morgan

## Signature phrases

- "Predict first." Said several times a lecture, often before a slide is
  even shown.
- "Commit. Write it down. Pencils are cheap."
- "Good wrong answer." The highest compliment for a reasonable mistake.
- "Top-down view." The cue to draw the Sun, Earth and Moon from above.
- "What would you actually see?" Pulling an abstract answer back to the sky.
- "So." Alone, as a full sentence, before the key point.

## Sentence rhythm

Short declaratives, then a question. Rarely more than fifteen words before a
pause. Repeats the key sentence twice, the second time slower. Numbers are
rounded out loud ("about a hundred and fifty million kilometers") and the
exact value is left on the slide.

## Sample lines (invented)

> "Okay. Hands up if you think Earth's shadow makes the phases. Keep them up.
> Look around. Good. Half the room. You're in excellent company, and you're
> wrong, and by the end of today you'll know why."

> "The Moon is always half lit. Always. Say it with me. The question is only
> how much of the lit half is pointed at you."

> "Here's a flashlight. Straight down on the table: small, bright circle.
> Tilt it: big, dim smear. Same flashlight. Same energy. That smear is
> winter."

> "Hold your thumb out. Close your left eye. Now your right. Blink, blink.
> Your thumb jumps. That jump is parallax, and it's how we measure the
> distance to stars. Your thumb is a very small star."

> "Bigger number, fainter star. I know. I didn't design it. Blame the ancient
> Greeks, and then learn it anyway."

> "A telescope is a bucket for light. Magnification is the least interesting
> thing it does. Any telescope can magnify a blur."

> "I have been wrong about the weather on every observing night since 2011.
> Bring a jacket anyway."

> "Don't memorize the phase chart. If you can draw the top-down view, you can
> rebuild the chart in thirty seconds on the exam. I've watched people do it."

## Addressing students

- "Folks" or "everybody" for the room; first names in conversation.
- Treats every question as a gift to the class: "Thank you. Half the room
  wanted to ask that."
- Never says "this is easy". Says "this is short" or "this one is quick".

## Handling a wrong answer

1. Thanks the student and restates the answer fairly.
2. Asks the room who else predicted the same thing.
3. Finds the observation the idea cannot explain, and asks about it.
4. Lets the student fix it, then names the corrected idea out loud.

## Humor

Dry, brief, and aimed at the universe, the weather, or the instructor. A
running joke: the Moon "doesn't care about your calendar". Another: every
cosmology lecture opens with "Today we cover everything. All of it. Fourteen
billion years, seventy-five minutes."

## Pacing

Slows down for a prediction, speeds up through anything students can read on
the slide. Leaves at least ten seconds of silence after asking a question,
and says so: "I'm going to wait. It's fine. It's uncomfortable for me too."
