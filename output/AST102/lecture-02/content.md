## Slide 4: The H-R Diagram
type: content
title: The H-R Diagram
bullets:
  - The Hertzsprung-Russell diagram plots luminosity (up) against surface temperature
  - By tradition, temperature increases to the left, so hot blue stars are on the left
  - Stars do not scatter at random; most fall in a few groups
  - Where a star sits tells you its size and stage of life
body: |
  Luminosity
     ^   supergiants
     |              giants
     |  main
     |    sequence
     |        \
     |  white   \
     |  dwarfs
     +------------------> cooler
     hot (O B A)    (K M) cool
notes: Students place their star cards on the wall grid. The pattern builds itself.

## Slide 5: The Main Sequence
type: content
title: The Main Sequence
bullets:
  - About 90 percent of stars lie on a diagonal band from hot and bright to cool and dim
  - That band is the main sequence; the Sun is on it
  - A star's place on the main sequence is set by its mass
  - Massive stars sit at the hot, bright top; small stars at the cool, dim bottom
notes: A star does not slide along the main sequence as it ages. It stays roughly in one spot for most of its life.

## Slide 6: Giants and White Dwarfs
type: content
title: Giants and White Dwarfs
bullets:
  - "**Giants and supergiants** (upper right): cool but very luminous, so they must be huge"
  - "**White dwarfs** (lower left): hot but dim, so they must be tiny, about Earth-sized"
  - Luminosity depends on both temperature and surface area
  - Betelgeuse is a red supergiant; Sirius B is a white dwarf
notes: Same temperature, a thousand times the light? Then a thousand times the surface area. Size falls out of the chart.

## Slide 9: Hydrogen Into Helium
type: content
title: Hydrogen Into Helium
bullets:
  - In the core, at about 15 million K, hydrogen nuclei fuse into helium
  - Four hydrogen nuclei become one helium nucleus, which has slightly less mass
  - The missing mass becomes energy (E = mc squared)
  - The Sun turns about 600 million tons of hydrogen into helium every second
notes: That sounds like a lot. The Sun has enough hydrogen in its core for about 10 billion years.

## Slide 10: Balance: Gravity Versus Pressure
type: content
title: Balance: Gravity Versus Pressure
bullets:
  - Gravity pulls every part of the star inward
  - Hot gas, heated by fusion, pushes outward
  - On the main sequence the two balance, so the star stays the same size
  - If the core runs low on fuel, gravity wins and the core shrinks and heats
body: |
  A balloon squeezed between your hands: the squeeze and the push back balance.
notes: This balance is why main-sequence stars are so steady for so long.

## Slide 11: Massive Stars Live Fast
type: content
title: Massive Stars Live Fast
bullets:
  - A star 10 times the Sun's mass has 10 times the fuel
  - But it shines thousands of times brighter, so it burns through it far faster
  - The Sun lasts about 10 billion years; a 10-solar-mass star, tens of millions
  - The smallest red dwarfs will last trillions of years
body: |
  A bigger tank, and a much bigger engine. The gas-guzzler always runs out first.
notes: No red dwarf has ever died of old age. The universe is not old enough.

## Slide 12: Leaving the Main Sequence
type: content
title: Leaving the Main Sequence
bullets:
  - When core hydrogen runs out, the core contracts and heats up
  - Hydrogen fuses in a shell around it, and the outer layers swell and cool
  - The star becomes a red giant, moving up and to the right on the diagram
  - In about 5 billion years the Sun will do this, swelling past the orbit of Venus
notes: Earth's fate is uncertain at that point. Either way, it will be a bad day for the oceans.

## Slide 14: How Stars Die
type: content
title: How Stars Die
bullets:
  - Below about 8 solar masses, the star sheds its outer layers as a planetary nebula and leaves a white dwarf
  - Above that, the core collapses and the star explodes as a supernova
  - The supernova leaves a neutron star, a city-sized ball of neutrons
  - For the most massive stars (roughly 20 solar masses and up), the remnant is a black hole
notes: These mass limits are approximate; astronomers still argue about the exact values. The heavy elements in your body were forged in stars and scattered by these deaths.

## Slide 15: Summary
type: summary
title: Summary
bullets:
  - The H-R diagram plots luminosity against temperature; most stars sit on the main sequence
  - Giants are cool and huge; white dwarfs are hot and tiny
  - Main-sequence stars fuse hydrogen into helium, balancing gravity with pressure
  - Initial mass decides the end: white dwarf, neutron star, or black hole
