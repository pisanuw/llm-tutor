## Slide 3: Predict: A Siren Passes
type: quiz
title: Predict: A Siren Passes
bullets:
  - An ambulance drives toward you with its siren on, passes, and drives away
  - What happens to the pitch as it approaches? As it moves away?
  - What is happening to the sound waves themselves?
  - If light did the same thing, what would happen to its color?
notes: Swing the buzzer on a string. Pitch up coming toward you, down going away. That is the Doppler effect.

## Slide 4: Redshift
type: content
title: Redshift
bullets:
  - Light from a source moving away from us is stretched to longer wavelengths
  - Spectral lines shift toward the red end of the spectrum: a redshift
  - "z = (observed wavelength - rest wavelength) / rest wavelength"
  - For small z, the recession speed is about z times the speed of light
body: |
  The pitch of a passing siren drops because its waves are stretched. Light does the same.
notes: We know the rest wavelengths from laboratory spectra, so a shifted line is a speedometer.

## Slide 5: Try It: Measure a Redshift
type: quiz
title: Try It: Measure a Redshift
bullets:
  - The hydrogen-alpha line has a rest wavelength of 656.3 nm
  - In a galaxy's spectrum it appears at 662.9 nm
  - Find z, then the galaxy's speed away from us
  - Sanity check: is that faster or slower than light? Should it be?
notes: z is 6.6 over 656.3, about 0.010. Speed is about 0.010 times 300,000 km/s, about 3,000 km/s.

## Slide 6: Stretching Space, Not Just Motion
type: content
title: Stretching Space, Not Just Motion
bullets:
  - For distant galaxies, the redshift is not motion through space
  - Space itself expands while the light travels, stretching it along the way
  - This is cosmological redshift; the farther the light came, the more it stretched
  - For nearby galaxies, the Doppler picture gives nearly the same answer
notes: Light that left a galaxy when the universe was half its present size arrives with its wavelengths doubled.

## Slide 7: Galaxies Rushing Away
type: content
title: Galaxies Rushing Away
bullets:
  - In the 1910s and 1920s, astronomers found most galaxies are redshifted
  - Once distances were measured, a pattern appeared
  - The farther a galaxy is, the faster it recedes
  - Only a few nearby galaxies, like Andromeda, are approaching
notes: Andromeda is coming toward our galaxy at about 110 km/s. Gravity wins locally.

## Slide 8: Hubble's Law
type: content
title: Hubble's Law
bullets:
  - "Recession velocity = Hubble constant x distance, or v = H0 x d"
  - H0 is about 70 km/s per megaparsec (1 megaparsec = 1 million parsecs)
  - Rearranged, d = v / H0 gives a distance from a measured redshift
  - Twice the distance, twice the speed
body: |
  A galaxy receding at 7,000 km/s is about 7,000 / 70 = 100 megaparsecs away.
notes: Be honest about H0: different methods give about 67 to 73, an open question right now. We use 70 for round numbers.

## Slide 10: No Center
type: content
title: No Center
bullets:
  - Picture raisins in a loaf of rising bread
  - Every raisin moves away from every other one
  - A raisin twice as far away moves away twice as fast: Hubble's law
  - Every raisin sees the same thing, so no raisin is the center
notes: The Big Bang was not an explosion at a point in space. It happened everywhere, and space itself is stretching. Galaxies themselves do not expand; gravity holds them together.

---

## Slide 11: The Age of the Universe
type: content
title: The Age of the Universe
bullets:
  - Run the expansion backward and the galaxies were once all together
  - The time since then is roughly 1 / H0
  - With H0 = 70 km/s/Mpc, that is about 14 billion years
  - Detailed measurements give 13.8 billion years
notes: The oldest stars we know are a bit younger than that. A good consistency check.

## Slide 12: The Evidence
type: content
title: The Evidence
bullets:
  - "**The expansion:** galaxies recede following Hubble's law"
  - "**The cosmic microwave background:** a faint glow from every direction, the cooled light of the hot early universe"
  - "**Light elements:** the measured amounts of hydrogen, helium and lithium match predictions for a hot, dense start"
  - Three independent lines of evidence point to the same history
notes: Ask which piece of evidence would convince them most. There is no wrong answer to that one.

## Slide 13: The Cosmic Microwave Background
type: content
title: The Cosmic Microwave Background
bullets:
  - About 380,000 years after the start, the universe cooled enough for light to travel freely
  - That light has been stretched by expansion into microwaves
  - Today it is the glow of something at about 2.7 K, nearly the same in every direction
  - Its tiny ripples are the seeds of today's galaxies
body: |
  A stove element glows red-hot, then cools to a glow only an infrared camera sees. Same idea, much bigger.
notes: The baby picture of the universe. A small part of the static on an old analog TV was this glow.

## Slide 15: Summary
type: summary
title: Summary
bullets:
  - Redshift z = (observed - rest) / rest; small z gives speed of about z times c
  - Hubble's law, v = H0 x d with H0 about 70 km/s/Mpc, turns a redshift into a distance
  - Space itself expands, with no center; 1 / H0 gives an age near 14 billion years
  - Evidence for the Big Bang: the expansion, the microwave background, the light elements
