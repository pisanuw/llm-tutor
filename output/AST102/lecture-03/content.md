## Slide 4: Our Galaxy
type: content
title: Our Galaxy
bullets:
  - The Milky Way is a flat disk of stars, gas and dust about 100,000 light-years across
  - A thicker bulge of older stars sits at the center
  - A faint, roughly spherical halo of old stars and globular clusters surrounds it all
  - It holds a few hundred billion stars; the Sun is one of them
body: |
  Standing in a forest you see trees all around the horizon and open sky overhead. That is the view from inside a disk.
notes: The band is the disk seen edge-on from within. Above and below the disk we look out through fewer stars.

---

## Slide 5: Where Are We?
type: content
title: Where Are We?
bullets:
  - The Sun is in the disk, about 26,000 light-years (8 kiloparsecs) from the center
  - It orbits the center at about 230 km/s
  - One orbit takes about 230 million years
  - The center lies toward the constellation Sagittarius, hidden behind dust
notes: The last time the Sun was where it is now in its orbit, the first dinosaurs were appearing.

---

## Slide 6: How We Found the Center
type: content
title: How We Found the Center
bullets:
  - Dust blocks visible light along the disk, so we cannot just look
  - Early star counts wrongly put the Sun near the center
  - Globular clusters, seen above and below the dust, are centered on a point far away in Sagittarius
  - Infrared and radio light pass through the dust and confirm it
notes: We are not at the center of our galaxy, just as Earth is not at the center of the solar system. Astronomy keeps moving us out of the middle.

## Slide 7: Other Galaxies
type: content
title: Other Galaxies
bullets:
  - The faint "nebulae" seen in early telescopes turned out to be whole galaxies
  - The Andromeda galaxy is about 2.5 million light-years away
  - The observable universe holds hundreds of billions of galaxies
  - Galaxies gather in groups and clusters; ours is in the Local Group
notes: Andromeda is the farthest thing most people can see with the naked eye.

## Slide 9: Spirals, Ellipticals, Irregulars
type: content
title: Spirals, Ellipticals, Irregulars
bullets:
  - "**Spiral:** a disk with arms, a central bulge, plenty of gas and dust (the Milky Way, Andromeda)"
  - "**Elliptical:** a smooth round or oval ball of stars with little gas or dust"
  - "**Irregular:** no clear shape, often rich in gas (the Magellanic Clouds)"
  - Some spirals have a straight bar through the center; ours does
notes: Shape is the first clue to a galaxy's history.

---

## Slide 10: Stars and Gas by Type
type: content
title: Stars and Gas by Type
bullets:
  - Spiral arms glow blue with young, hot stars forming from gas
  - Ellipticals look yellow-red; their stars are old and star formation has mostly stopped
  - Irregulars are often bursting with new stars
  - Gas is the raw material; no gas, no new stars
notes: Massive blue stars live only millions of years. If you see them, stars formed recently. Connects back to lecture 02.

## Slide 12: The Solar System Comparison
type: content
title: The Solar System Comparison
bullets:
  - Almost all the solar system's mass is in the Sun
  - So the farther a planet is, the weaker the pull and the slower its orbit
  - Mercury moves at about 47 km/s; Neptune at about 5 km/s
  - Plotted against distance, orbital speed falls off steadily
body: |
  Swing a ball on a string. To swing it faster at the same radius you have to pull harder.
notes: Orbit speed tells you how hard gravity pulls, and the pull tells you how much mass is inside the orbit.

## Slide 13: Rotation Curves
type: content
title: Rotation Curves
bullets:
  - A rotation curve plots orbital speed against distance from a galaxy's center
  - If the visible stars and gas were all the mass, speeds would fall off far out
  - Instead, speeds stay roughly flat out to the edge of the visible disk and beyond
  - So the outer stars feel the pull of much more mass than we can see
body: |
  speed
    ^      observed: flat
    |   ________________________
    |  /     .
    | /         .   expected from visible matter
    |/              .     .
    +-----------------------------> distance from center
notes: This was measured in galaxy after galaxy, mostly from the radio glow of hydrogen gas far out in the disk.

## Slide 14: Dark Matter
type: content
title: Dark Matter
bullets:
  - The unseen mass is called dark matter; it does not emit, absorb or reflect light
  - Galaxies sit inside large halos of it
  - There is about five times as much dark matter as ordinary matter
  - What it is made of is still unknown; it does not seem to be dark gas, dust or dead stars
notes: Be honest about this one. The evidence that something is there is strong; what it is remains open.

## Slide 15: Summary
type: summary
title: Summary
bullets:
  - The Milky Way is a disk with a bulge and a halo; we see the disk as a band
  - The Sun is about 26,000 light-years from the center, orbiting at about 230 km/s
  - Spirals have gas and young stars; ellipticals are old and gas-poor; irregulars have no clear shape
  - Flat rotation curves reveal dark matter, about five times the ordinary matter
